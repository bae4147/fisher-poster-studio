Closer to the leader, above the coworkers?
How leader gratitude shapes responses across relational targets

Hun Whee Lee, Yahua Cai, Yuanmeng Wang, Seo-Young Byun, Sang Hoon Han & Timothy Judge · Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 197 (2026), 104538

1Research narrative▶ listen

A thank-you from the boss is aimed at one person, but the whole team sees it.

Gratitude research mostly looks at the leader and one employee. This paper asks what the same thank-you does to the employee's view of their coworkers.

It combines two theories. Social exchange: thanks is an investment, so people return it to the leader. Social comparison: thanks also signals "you stand out," so some people use it to rank themselves above their peers.

2Key findings▶ listen

Road 1 held in all 4 studies. Road 2 never opened on average, only for people high in Machiavellianism (4 of 4 studies). Prosocial motivation strengthened Road 1 in the two field studies only.

2bWho reads thanks as a ranking? Effect of gratitude on "I'm above my coworkers"▶ listen

Simple slopes (b) of gratitude on sense of superiority. Red bars were significant in every study; grey bars never were. Rating scales differ across studies, so compare the direction, not the size.

3Research methods: four studies, two kinds of evidence▶ listen

Study 1 · China524 manufacturing employees, three waves a month apart. Supervisors rated helping and coworkers rated harmful behavior.field · multi-source
Study 2 · US180 full-time employees (Prolific), two surveys a week apart.field · replication
Study 3 · Experiment138 students with work experience read a scenario about "Sam," randomly assigned to high or low thanks.vignette · pre-registered
Study 4 · UK142 full-time employees recalled a real episode of being thanked, or a neutral talk with their boss.recall · pre-registered

Field studies are realistic but can't show cause. The experiments show cause but are simplified. Used together, each covers the other's weakness.

4Practical takeaway▶ listen

Keep thanking people. Pay attention to how you frame it and who hears it.

Frame it as a team win. When you praise one person's work, also name the people who supported it.
Train both sides. Teach people to give and receive thanks gracefully, without ranking anyone.
Watch both relationships. Track how thanks affects the bond with the leader and the relationships among peers.
